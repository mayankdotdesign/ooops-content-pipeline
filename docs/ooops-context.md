# Ooops — Context for the Instagram Content Pipeline

**Purpose of this file:** this is the reference doc for the automated content pipeline (Claude Code) that generates relatable couples content for @ooops.app on Instagram. Read this before researching topics, writing captions, or generating post copy — it tells you what Ooops is, how to talk about it, and what to avoid.

---

## 1. What Ooops is

Ooops is a mobile-first couples accountability app (iOS, pre-launch, 2026). Instead of arguing over small relationship slip-ups, couples log them into a shared virtual jar. At the end of the month, whoever "owes" more settles up — symbolically, not with real money.

**Positioning (use this language, it's the anchor — earned 51 upvotes on Reddit and tested well):**
> "Not a scorecard. A running joke you both laugh at."

**Subheadline:**
> "Cash, coffee, or just keeping score — you make the rules."

**One-sentence pitch:**
> Ooops turns relationship slip-ups into a shared ritual — because laughing about it is always better than fighting.

**Important nuance:** there is no payment gateway. Nobody actually transfers money through the app. The ritual and the running tally are what matter — settlement can be real money, coffee, chores, or just bragging rights. Don't write copy that implies Ooops processes payments.

### Origin story (useful for founder/relatable content)
Mayank (the founder) kept a physical notepad with his girlfriend during a long-distance relationship between Indian cities, tracking offenses owed to her — it reached ₹18,000 in two months. That real, lived habit is where the app idea came from. This is a strong authentic angle for content — a real couple actually did this before it was an app.

## 2. Audience — who this content is for

- **Primary target for the Instagram pipeline specifically: English-speaking couples, 22–32, skewing US/UK/Canada.** This is a deliberate choice — Western audiences are more likely to convert to a paid app, so IG content should default to Western/English relatable scenarios and phrasing, not Indian-specific references, unless a post is explicitly for a different channel.
- Long-distance relationships (LDR) are an especially strong niche — high relatability, high Reddit engagement, a specific and underserved pain point. **But LDR is one segment of the primary audience, not the whole thing** (corrected 2026-09-17 after a batch skewed too LDR-heavy — 6 of 9 posts were LDR-specific). The jar mechanic is just as relevant to couples who live together or see each other daily — shared chores, gaming/screen time, who-forgot-what, meeting-the-parents, etc. **Every batch should mix co-located/general-couple content in alongside LDR, not default to LDR as the assumed scenario.**
- Secondary audience (not the IG content priority): Indian couples, LDR and co-located, 22–35.
- **Not the audience:** couples who share finances, or anyone who'd treat the jar as punitive/real financial tracking rather than a bit.

## 3. Core features (for accurate content — don't invent features)

### The Jar (home screen)
A glass jar that visually fills with coins as offenses get logged. Both partners' balances shown side by side. A live "who owes whom" indicator. Jar themes change by month/milestone.

### Offense Logger
Fast (under 5 seconds), playful, never accusatory. Preset categories + emoji, amount entry, coin-drop animation, optional funny note or voice memo. Partner gets a witty push notification the moment it happens.

### Monthly Settlement
End of month, net balance is calculated automatically. Animated "Jar Reveal" with confetti. Symbolic "Pay Up" acknowledgment (optional real UPI/GPay link for India — Ooops itself never touches the money).

### Monthly Settle Card
An auto-generated shareable image after every settlement: offenses logged, amount owed, top category, Ooops branding. Built for WhatsApp/Instagram Stories sharing. **This is the primary viral/growth mechanic** — 12 shareable moments a year, generated on-device.

### Offense Library
12 default gender-neutral starter categories (see below), fully custom categories, and future community-voted packs (Gamer Pack, WFH Pack, etc).

### Notifications
Warm, witty copy — never alert-sounding. e.g. *"Your boo added something to the jar... probably nothing 👀"* Never reveals the specific offense or amount in the notification itself (privacy).

### Streaks & Badges
Light gamification, no guilt: "Peaceful Week" (7 days no offenses), "Balanced Couple" (within 10% at month end), "First Settle."

### Couple Wrapped (annual, ships at month 12)
Spotify-Wrapped-style yearly recap. Personality-style output like "The Dish Avoiders" or "The Eternal Latecomers." Shareable Instagram Stories card.

### Premium — Ooops Plus ($4.99/mo, $29.99/yr, founding price $2.99/mo for first 100 users)
Custom jar themes, unlimited custom categories, advanced insights, partner activity feed, export/archive, priority reminders, gift subscriptions.

## 5. Brand voice & tone — the rules that matter most for content

Ooops is: **Warm · Playful · Intimate · Satisfying.** It should feel like a warm hug, not a spreadsheet or a court record.

| Principle | What it means for content |
|---|---|
| Warm, not clinical | Conversational, soft, never transactional-sounding |
| Playful, not childish | Funny, emoji-rich, but not cartoonish or try-hard |
| Intimate, not public | Content should feel like eavesdropping on a real couple, not a brand shouting |
| Satisfying, not addictive | No guilt-trip or dark-pattern framing, ever |
| Mutual, not accusatory | Never "gotcha" or one-sided. Both partners are equally the joke. |
| Trustworthy, not extractive | No ads, no data selling — this is a real product principle, not just a tagline, and can be referenced honestly if relevant |

**Tone target:** conversational, warm, self-deprecating, natural — never stiff, performative, or "smart-ass." Content grounded in real Reddit research and real relationship dynamics, not invented scenarios.

**The core objection to defuse:** people's first reaction is "this sounds like scorekeeping." The reframe that works is *"a running joke, not a scorecard"* — lead with that framing whenever content touches on the jar mechanic directly.

## 6. Hard content boundaries — never do these

These are product-level content safety rules (from the offense category guidelines) and apply equally to any social content, not just in-app categories:

- **Never** reference financial control or coercion (withholding money, controlling a partner's spending)
- **Never** reference or joke about physical or emotional abuse
- **Never** treat mental health (anxiety, depression, etc.) as a punishable "offense"
- **Never** touch fidelity, cheating, flirting, or jealousy — these are real issues, not jar-worthy pettiness
- **Never** reference a partner's body, weight, or appearance
- **Never** target gender, sexuality, religion, ethnicity, or any protected characteristic
- **Never** frame content around something that should actually be a real conversation, not a bit

**The test for any offense/scenario used in content:** is it petty (not serious)? Could both partners plausibly do it to each other? Would you laugh seeing it on a shared card? If any of those fail, don't use it.

Also avoid: implying real money changes hands through the app, implying the app is currently downloadable (it's pre-launch/waitlist), and implying one partner "reports" on the other — it should always read as mutual and equal.

## 8. Two content tracks (for planning/research)

1. **Couple/User POV** — Instagram Reels, relatable scenarios, carousels drawn from real relationship dynamics (this is the IG pipeline's main lane)
2. **Builder/Founder POV** — build-in-public angle from Mayank's own journey (secondary, mostly for other channels like Reddit/Twitter, but can crossover)
